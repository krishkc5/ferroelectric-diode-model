1. **Barrier heights still too high.** Schottky-Mott formula (W_F − χ) gives Ti/AlScN ≈ 2.33 eV;

   fit too sensitive at moderate V without bounded trap-density and m* parameters.
   Wave-3 showed re-enabling without those bounds makes the fit fail to converge.

## Wave-4 items, ranked by expected impact

### W4-1 — Explicit Schottky barrier table (Punches 9–11)  ⬅ highest leverage

**Why first:** every mechanism that scales with exp(−Φ_B/kT) (TE, WKB prefactor, PF
through the IL) is wrong until the barrier heights are right. Punches 9–11 were not
addressed in Waves 1–3. Fixing them is a prerequisite for a meaningful Wave-4 fit.

**What to do:**

Add an optional `phi_b_override_ev` field to `MetalElectrode` (or a separate
`interface_phi_b_ev` dict on `TransportFerroelectricDiode`). When set, it bypasses the
Schottky-Mott `W_F − χ` formula in `_barriers_ev`.

Target values (from `docs/parameter_audit_20260506.md` punches 9–11):

| Interface | W_F − χ (current) | Literature Φ_B | Primary citation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ti / HfO_x | 2.03 eV | 1.2–1.4 eV | Afanas'ev et al., APL 81, 1053 (2002), Fig. 3 |
| Ti / AlScN | 2.33 eV | 1.5–2.0 eV | Schönweger et al., AFM 32, 2109632 (2022), Fig. S6 |
| Al / HfO_x | 1.98 eV | 2.5–2.8 eV | Yeo et al., JAP 92, 7266 (2002), Table I |
| Al / AlScN | 2.28 eV | 1.4–1.8 eV | Casamento et al., APL 120, 152901 (2022), Fig. 4 |
| Pd / AlScN | 3.30 eV | 2.8–3.2 eV | Liu et al., IEEE EDL 42, 1452 (2021), p. 1454 |

**Code changes required:**
- `src/material_types.py`: add `phi_b_override_ev: float | None = None` to `MetalElectrode`.
- `src/transport_fed.py` or `src/transport.py` (`_barriers_ev`): when `phi_b_override_ev`
  is set on the electrode facing the FE (or IL), use it instead of the computed barrier.
- `src/materials.py`: set override values for the five interfaces above.
- `tests/`: add a test that verifies the override is applied and bypasses W_F − χ.
- Audit (`docs/audit_wave4_*.md`): adversarial check on barrier magnitudes.

**Expected outcome:** mean barriers drop by ~0.5–0.8 eV. TE is no longer suppressed by
30 OOM. The BG channel becomes a smaller fraction of total current at moderate V, giving
the optimizer room to fit shape rather than magnitude.

### W4-2 — Constrain and re-enable tunneling / TAT

**Why second:** once barriers are in the right range, tunneling through the IL becomes
the likely dominant low-bias mechanism (as in most thin-oxide Schottky stacks). TAT
through HfO_x oxygen vacancies is well-documented (Vandelli, Larcher, Bersuker group).

**What to do:**

1. Add `trap_density_per_m3: float` and `trap_capture_cross_section_m2: float` to
   `TransportModelParameters`. Defaults: 10²⁵ m⁻³ and 10⁻¹⁹ m² (mid-range O-vacancy,
   Foster PRB 65, 174117 (2002); Bersuker et al., JAP 110, 124518 (2011)).

2. Clarify the DOS m* vs. tunneling m* distinction in `TransportModelParameters`.
   Currently `src/transport.py` uses the same m_eff for the Richardson prefactor and
   the WKB exponent. Split into `dos_m_eff` (stays at 0.3/0.11) and
   `tunneling_m_eff_il` / `tunneling_m_eff_fe` (literature: HfO_x 0.10–0.20 m₀,
   AlScN 0.20–0.30 m₀; `docs/parameter_audit_20260506.md` rows 13, 14).

3. Add `trap_density_per_m3` and both `tunneling_m_eff_*` to the fit vector in
   `src/fit_dciv_experiment.py` with literature bounds.

4. Re-enable `enable_tunneling=True` and `enable_trap_assisted_tunneling=True`.

5. Run Wave-4 fit; expect cost to decrease and BG to shrink.

**Tests:** update `tests/test_stack_aware_transport.py` to verify tunneling current is
non-zero when enabled and scales correctly with IL thickness.

### W4-5 — Tung barrier-height distribution on the tunneling channel

`docs/refit_20260507.md` item 2. More complex than the TE Tung enhancement (Wave 3)
because the WKB exponent, not just the prefactor, varies across patches.

Reference: Sullivan et al., JAP 70, 7403 (1991).

The average WKB transmission through a Gaussian distribution of barrier-height patches:

```
⟨T_WKB⟩ = ∫ T_WKB(φ̄ + δφ) · P(δφ; σ) dδφ
```

where `T_WKB(φ) = exp(−(4π/h) ∫ √(2m*(φ(x)−E)) dx)`. Unlike the TE case the
integral does not have a closed form; it requires either Monte Carlo sampling over
barrier patches or a second-order expansion `⟨T⟩ ≈ T(φ̄) · exp(σ² · (d²ln T/dφ²)/2)`.

Defer to after W4-1 and W4-2 are fitted and stable; the tunneling distribution is
relevant only once the baseline tunneling current is correct.

### W4-6 — Landau–Khalatnikov (LK) dynamics layer

`docs/refit_20260507.md` item 5. Adds time-dependence to the domain switching; relevant
for explaining sweep-rate-dependent coercive fields and any frequency-dependent
measurements. Largest scope among all Wave-4 items.

Prerequisite: the quasistatic DC fit must first converge well (W4-1/W4-2). LK dynamics
add a viscosity coefficient `ρ_LK` and require time-stepping through the voltage
sweep rather than a static per-bias-point solution.
