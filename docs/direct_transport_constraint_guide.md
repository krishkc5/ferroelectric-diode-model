# Direct transport constraint guide

The segmented direct fitter in `src/fit_direct_transport_equations.py` is now set up to use named constraint presets. The goal is to stop treating every parameter as unconstrained and instead move toward a workflow in which literature-backed ranges are supplied first and the optimizer only explores within those ranges.

The global transport constants fitted by the direct model are, in order, `top_phi_ev`, `top_gamma`, `log10_top_prefactor_a`, `bottom_phi_ev`, `bottom_gamma`, `log10_bottom_prefactor_a`, `pf_trap_depth_ev`, `pf_fe_beta`, `log10_pf_fe_prefactor_a_per_v`, `pf_il_beta`, `log10_pf_il_prefactor_a_per_v`, `log10_bg_cond`, and `log10_i_floor`. The segment weights are fitted separately and are best treated as effective branch-allocation factors rather than literature observables.

Three presets are currently available in `src/direct_transport_constraints.py`. The `broad` preset is intentionally loose and is mainly useful for exploratory fits. The `physics-reasonable` preset is the current default and is meant to be a first-pass range set for the `Ti | HfOx | AlScN | Al` stack. The `literature-ready` preset is a placeholder for the next stage, where the bounds are tightened after literature review.

The parameters most worth checking in literature first are the quantities that can plausibly correspond to material or interface physics rather than purely effective fitting knobs. The first group is the pair of effective injection barriers, `top_phi_ev` and `bottom_phi_ev`. These should be checked against reported or inferred electron injection barriers for the `Ti / HfOx` and `Al / AlScN` interfaces. The second group is the trap depth `pf_trap_depth_ev`, which should be compared against trap activation energies reported for `HfOx`, `AlScN`, or closely related nitride/oxide stacks. The third group is the rough field-lowering sensitivity represented in the current compact fit by `top_gamma`, `bottom_gamma`, `pf_fe_beta`, and `pf_il_beta`. These are not usually reported directly in the same form, but literature on image-force barrier lowering and PF slopes can still be used to determine whether the current ranges are wildly too small or too large.

The parameters that should usually not be treated as direct literature targets are the thermionic prefactors, the PF prefactors, the background conductance, the current floor, and the segment weights. Those are better interpreted as effective compact-model amplitudes that absorb geometry-specific details, contact area uncertainties, pinning, roughness, and other missing physics.

If you are collecting literature constraints for the next round, the most useful checklist is: electron affinity or conduction-band offset for `HfOx` and `AlScN`; work functions for `Ti` and `Al`; electron injection barriers for `Ti / HfOx` and `Al / AlScN`; trap activation energies or PF activation energies in `HfOx` and `AlScN`; dielectric constants of `HfOx` and `AlScN`; and any evidence of interface pinning or Fermi-level pinning at either contact. Once those are in hand, the `literature-ready` preset can be replaced with stack-specific lower and upper bounds.
