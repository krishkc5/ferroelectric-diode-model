## C. DIPOLE MAGNITUDE / SCREENING REALISM — the open question

### C.1 Numerical evaluation at AlScN-scale parameters

Pulling the live values from `src/materials.py`:
- `AlScN.p_r = AtomicUnits.convert_polarization(110)` = 110 µC/cm² = **1.10 C/m²**
  (`materials.py:180`).
- Ti screening length: `screening_len=AtomicUnits.nm_to_bohr(0.045)` = 0.045 nm
  = **4.5 × 10⁻¹¹ m** (`materials.py:99`).
- Al screening length: same 0.045 nm (`materials.py:129`).
- Ti `k = 1`, no `eps_inf` field (`materials.py:100`); `MetalElectrode` class
  has no `eps_inf` slot in `material_types.py:1–18`. **Therefore
  `top_eps_inf` falls back to `top_electrode.k = 1`** via
  `transport_fed.py:39: getattr(top_electrode, "eps_inf", None) or top_electrode.k`.
  Same for Al.

Apply Eq. 4 (= |Δφ_B|/q for full Pr):
```
ΔV_screen = σ_pol · λ_TF / (ε0 · κ_∞,metal)
          = (1.10 C/m²) · (4.5 × 10⁻¹¹ m) / ((8.854 × 10⁻¹² F/m) · 1.0)
          = (4.95 × 10⁻¹¹) / (8.854 × 10⁻¹²)
          ≈ 5.59 V
```

So the **predicted dipole shift in this implementation is ≈ ±5.6 eV** at full
Pr. T2 reports `top up=7.65 eV` and `bottom down=7.36 eV`; subtracting the
≈ 2.3 eV base barrier (W − χ) leaves ≈ +5.4 eV and −5.0 eV (the latter
is then clamped to 0; see §D). These are consistent with 5.6 eV at the
reported-in-T2 polarization (which is the Preisach-mean, slightly below Pr).

### C.2 Comparison to FTJ literature

| Reference | System | Pr (µC/cm²) | λ_TF (Å) | κ_∞,metal | Predicted ΔV (V) | Reported ΔV (V) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tsymbal–Kohlstedt (2006), Fig. 2 | BTO/Pt vs. SrRuO₃ | ~26 | ~0.5–0.7 (SRO ~ 1.5) | 1 (Pt), ~7 (SRO eff.) | ~0.15–0.25 | 0.05–0.3 |
| Pantel & Alexe (2010), §III | BFO/Co/Pt | ~50 | ~0.5 (Pt), ~1.5 (Co) | 1 (Pt) | ~0.28 | 0.1–0.5 |
| Wave-2 Ti/AlScN/Al (this code) | AlScN | **110** | **0.45** | **1** (both) | **~5.6** | (model output) |

The literature systems sit at ~0.1–0.5 V because Pr is 2–4× smaller AND the
asymmetry between electrodes (asymmetric κ_∞,metal in Pt/Co or Pt/SrRuO₃
stacks) cuts the effective shift on at least one side. The current AlScN
parameters give a literal-formula prediction ≈ 10–30× larger than any
literature value, which is the heart of the §C question.

### C.3 Root-cause judgment

The 5.6 V output is a **legitimate evaluation of the linear
Tsymbal–Kohlstedt formula at AlScN parameters** but is **not a physical
prediction** — for three compounding reasons:

1. **AlScN polarization is in the regime where the linear Thomas–Fermi
   formula breaks down.** At σ_pol ≈ 1.1 C/m², the screening charge per unit
   area is ≈ 7 × 10¹⁸ electrons/m², which over a 0.45 Å Thomas–Fermi length
   corresponds to a *volume* density of ≈ 10²⁹/m³ — equal to or above the
   metal's free-electron density n0 = 7 × 10²⁸/m³ (`materials.py:98, 127`).
   Linear screening assumes the induced charge is a small perturbation on
   n0; here it is comparable. Real metals **cannot screen 1.1 C/m² in a
   0.45 Å sheet** — the screening charge spreads further into the metal,
   and/or the electrostatic potential drop saturates at the Fermi-level
   pinning floor (typically ~ 1 V before the metal's d-band crosses E_F).
   This is the **nonlinear-screening saturation** issue (root-cause **iii**
   in the prompt), and it dominates here. AlScN is not BTO; the linear
   formula was derived for δσ_pol ≪ n0 · λ_TF · q, which is satisfied for
   BTO (~26 µC/cm²) and BFO (~50 µC/cm²) but **violated for AlScN at 110
   µC/cm²**.

2. **The κ_∞,metal = 1 choice is technically defensible but practically
   wrong.** The contract (`polarization_barrier_coupling.md` §3, "Why κ_∞
   not κ_static") justifies κ_∞ ~ 1 with a Drude-tail argument. This is
   correct in the IR limit. **But the relevant timescale for static
   polarization screening is the Thomas–Fermi response time,
   τ_TF ~ 1/ω_p ~ 0.1 fs**, which sits well above ω_p (Ti: ~ 4 eV, Al:
   ~ 15 eV); for a *static* dipole sheet, the metal's screening response
   includes the bound-electron contribution and **κ_∞,eff is in the range
   2–10 for Ti and 1.5–3 for Al** (depending on the energy at which one
   reads ε_metal(ω)). The authors who use the κ_∞,metal ≈ 1 form (e.g.
   Tsymbal–Kohlstedt Fig. 2 caption) implicitly assume an effective
   *much-larger* κ when comparing to data, by tuning λ_TF to absorb the
   factor. Real ab-initio screening calculations (Stengel et al. PRB 2009,
   Nature Mater. 2009 § "interface chemistry") show effective screening
   lengths 5–10× larger than the literal Thomas–Fermi value when interface
   chemistry is included — this is a **κ_∞,metal parameter-choice issue**
   (root-cause **ii**), and it compounds with #1.

3. **The contract's λ_TF = 0.45 Å is at the short edge of the literature
   range** (`docs/parameter_audit_20260506.md` rows 21–22 cite 0.5–0.7 Å for
   Ti, 0.05 nm for Al). This is a 10–30 % effect — small relative to #1
   and #2, but it nudges in the same direction.

**Verdict:** The 5+ V output is **NOT a numerical bug** — the implementation
faithfully encodes Eq. 4–6 with the parameters stipulated by the contract.
But the **linear Tsymbal–Kohlstedt formula is being applied outside its
domain of validity for AlScN-scale Pr.** The clamp at `max(barrier, 0.0)`
in `transport.py:175–188` (see §D) is what keeps the model from producing
nonsense currents — but it is a band-aid that hides the underlying physics
breakdown. **Root-cause is primarily (iii) nonlinear-screening saturation,
secondarily (ii) κ_∞,metal parameter choice.**

### C.4 Recommended remediation (for Wave-3 F-REFIT)

Two paths, in priority order:

**Path A (preferred): replace linear Eq. 4 with a nonlinear saturation
form.** The Tsymbal–Kohlstedt 2006 paper (Eq. 1) and Stengel et al. (2009)
both note that for high-Pr ferroelectrics the screening dipole saturates at
the metal's *Fermi-level pinning* floor, typically 1.0–1.5 V (set by the
metal d-band offset to the dielectric CBM). A practical replacement:
```
ΔV_screen_sat = ΔV_pin · tanh( σ_pol · λ_TF / (ε0 · κ_∞,metal · ΔV_pin) )
```
with `ΔV_pin ≈ 1.0 V` (Ti/AlScN; Schönweger AFM 2022). For small σ_pol this
recovers the linear form; for large σ_pol it saturates near ΔV_pin. Wire
this into `_polarization_dipole_shift_ev` at `transport.py:100–121`. This
removes the need for the `max(barrier, 0.0)` clamp in §D.

**Path B (interim): treat κ_∞,metal as a fittable parameter and bump it to
~5–10.** This is what BTO/BFO papers implicitly do. Add an
`eps_inf` field to `MetalElectrode` (`material_types.py:1–18`) and populate
per-metal: Ti ≈ 5.0, Al ≈ 3.0 (Drude-tail in the relevant energy range
~ 1–10 eV; Mehta–Silverman–Jacobs 1973 §III suggests this range). With
κ_∞,Ti = 5: ΔV ≈ 5.6/5 = 1.1 V — already inside the literature band. This
is a parameter fix only; no formula change.

**Either fix renders the §D clamp harmless (it would never trigger for any
realistic input) and would resolve the T1 failure indirectly: the WF
asymmetry would be a larger fraction of the barrier height because the
dipole shift wouldn't dominate.**

**Cross-cutting recommendation:** document explicitly in the contract that
AlScN is a different operating regime than BTO/BFO; even with Path A or B,
the predicted on/off ratios will be 100× larger than BTO FTJs (which is
consistent with experimental AlScN diode data: Schönweger AFM 2022 reports
~ 10⁵× ON/OFF, vs. ~10² for BTO).

## D. CLAMPING / DOMAIN-OF-VALIDITY GUARDS

### D.1 The clamp is real and is a `max(..., 0.0)` on the barrier

`src/transport.py:175–181` (top barrier):
```python
top_barrier = max(
    top_base_barrier
    + delta_phi_top_ev
    - self.parameters.thermionic_top_schottky_scale
    * self._schottky_lowering_ev(top_field_v_per_m, top_image_kappa),
    0.0,
)
```

`src/transport.py:182–188` (bottom barrier):
```python
bottom_barrier = max(
    bottom_base_barrier
    + delta_phi_bot_ev
    - self.parameters.thermionic_bottom_schottky_scale
    * self._schottky_lowering_ev(bottom_field_v_per_m, self.diode.fe_eps_inf),
    0.0,
)
```

A second pair of clamps at L202–207 (after offset/floor adjustment) and
mirrored in `audit_state` at L467–472 — same `max(..., 0.0)` form.

### D.2 What it does and why it triggers

For P > 0 (up), Eq. 5/6 give:
- Δφ_top ≈ +5.6 eV → top barrier ≈ 2.33 + 5.6 − 0.06 ≈ +7.87 eV (uncliamped;
  T2 reports 7.65 eV — small differences from polarization < Pr after solver).
- Δφ_bot ≈ −5.6 eV → bottom barrier ≈ 2.28 − 5.6 ≈ **−3.3 eV** before clamp;
  the `max(..., 0.0)` clamps it to **0.00 eV**, exactly matching T2's
  `bot up=0.00 eV, down=7.36 eV` print-out.

### D.3 Verdict and flag

**PASS as a code clamp** (negative Schottky barriers are unphysical in the
TE injection model — the Boltzmann factor `exp(-φ_B/kT)` would yield
`> Richardson floor`, which is itself wrong because Schottky-Richardson
TE no longer applies once the barrier crosses the Fermi level — the
correct regime is degenerate Ohmic).

**FAIL as physics-modeling**: the clamp papers over the §C nonlinear-
screening issue by silently truncating Δφ at the unphysical −2.28 eV
threshold instead of using a saturation form (Path A in §C.4). When the
clamp triggers, *all polarization-magnitude information past the clamp
threshold is discarded* — switching P from 110 µC/cm² to 80 µC/cm² produces
the same 0.00 eV bottom barrier, so the model loses sensitivity to the
Preisach hysteresis details for the dominant electrode.

**User-flag:** the clamp is currently load-bearing for keeping T2's
electroresistance ratio at a sensible 4.58 decades rather than diverging.
Fix the §C nonlinear-screening issue first; only then reconsider whether
the clamp is still needed.

## F. T1 FAILURE DIAGNOSIS

### F.1 Restate the analytic baseline

T1 sets up Ti/AlScN/Al with **no IL** (`il_nm=0.0`), 10 nm AlScN, P_net ≈ 0.
The expected pure-TE log-asymmetry at ±2 V is built into the test:
```python
delta_wf = 4.33 - 4.28  # eV
analytic_log_ratio = delta_wf / (kT_eV * math.log(10))
```
With kT = 0.02585 eV, ln(10) = 2.3026:
`analytic_log_ratio = 0.05 / (0.02585 × 2.3026) = 0.05 / 0.05953 = 0.840`. ✓
This confirms the printout.

### F.2 Is TE actually dominant at ±2 V over 10 nm AlScN?

Trace the relative magnitudes of the four channels at +2 V, P = 0:
- **Base barrier**: top = bottom = W − χ ≈ 4.30 − 2.0 = 2.30 eV (no σ_pol shift
  at P = 0; `_polarization_dipole_shift_ev` returns 0).
- **Image-force lowering** at E ≈ 2 V / 10 nm = 2 × 10⁸ V/m, with κ_∞ = 4.6
  for AlScN (since `il_nm = 0` → fallback to `fe_eps_inf` per
  `transport.py:172–174`):
  Δφ_IF = √(qE/(4π ε0 κ_∞)) = √((1.6e-19)(2e8) / ((4π)(8.85e-12)(4.6)))
        = √(3.2e-11 / 5.12e-10) = √(0.0625) ≈ **0.25 V** → barrier ≈ 2.05 eV.
- **TE current**:
  J_TE = A* T² exp(−φ_B/kT) = (1.2 × 10⁶)(1)(300²) × exp(−2.05 / 0.02585)
       = 1.08 × 10¹¹ × exp(−79.3) = 1.08 × 10¹¹ × 4 × 10⁻³⁵
       ≈ **4 × 10⁻²⁴ A/m²**. Effectively zero.

- **Tunneling current** (DT/WKB):
  WKB exponent for a 2.3 eV barrier across 10 nm with m* = 0.3:
  α = √(2 m* m_e φ_B) / ℏ = √(2 · 0.3 · 9.11e-31 · 2.3 · 1.6e-19) / 1.054e-34
    = √(2.01e-49) / 1.054e-34 = 4.49e-25 / 1.054e-34 = 4.26 × 10⁹ m⁻¹.
  exponent = 2 α t = 2 × 4.26e9 × 1e-8 = 85.2 → transmission ≈ exp(−85) = 1.2e−37
  (similar to TE — but with field tilt at 2 V / 10 nm the effective
  trapezoidal area is reduced by ~50 %, giving T ≈ exp(−42) ≈ 6e−19).
  Carrier flux prefactor: `_carrier_flux ≈ 0.25 q n v_F` ~ 0.25 × 1.6e-19
  × 7e28 × 1.4e6 ≈ 4 × 10¹⁵ A/m². Multiplied by transmission ≈ 6e−19 →
  J_DT ≈ **2 × 10⁻³ A/m²** — orders of magnitude bigger than TE but still
  small, because barrier is ~2 eV wide.
- **TAT**: prefactor `5e8` × transmission with reduced barrier (φ − trap_depth_AlScN
  = 2.3 − 0.8 = 1.5 eV), shorter exponent → T_TAT ≈ exp(−27) ≈ 2e−12 →
  J_TAT ≈ **1 × 10⁻³ A/m²** (similar to DT).
- **PF**: trap depth 0.8 eV minus Frenkel lowering ≈ 0.5 V (E ~ 2e8 V/m,
  κ_∞ = 4.6, π form):
  √(qE/(π ε0 κ_∞)) = 2 × √(qE/(4π ε0 κ_∞)) = 2 × 0.25 ≈ **0.5 V**.
  Activation = 0.8 − 0.5 = 0.3 eV → exp(−0.3/0.02585) ≈ 1e−5.
  J_PF ≈ q n_t μ E × exp(...) ≈ (1.6e-19)(5e23)(1e-10)(2e8)(1e-5) ≈
  **1.6 × 10⁻³ A/m²**.
- **SCLC**: (9/8) ε0 κ μ V² / d³ with d=1e-8, V=2 → 1.125 × 8.854e-12 × 18 ×
  5e-10 × 4 / 1e-24 = ~3.6 × 10⁻¹ A/m² ~ **0.36 A/m²**.

Order: SCLC > {DT, PF, TAT} >> TE.

The test reports `J_pos = 2.5 × 10⁵ A/m²` which is 10⁵–10⁷× larger than
my hand estimates. This means I've under-estimated something — most
likely SCLC (its mobility/prefactor) or DT (the carrier-flux
formulation). Without running code I can't get the exact number, but
**the ranking is robust: TE is tiny (10⁻²⁴) compared to every other
channel, by 20+ orders of magnitude.**

### F.3 The user's hypothesis is correct: TE does not dominate at ±2 V

At ±2 V over 10 nm AlScN with no IL, the dominant channels are
{tunneling, PF, TAT, SCLC} — all of which depend on the *signed* E_FE
(via `_field_sign`, see B.6/7/8). At P = 0, E_FE has no
polarization-driven offset, so it is set entirely by the 0.05 eV WF
difference giving a built-in field of order
`(W_Ti − W_Al) / t_FE ≈ 0.05 V / 10 nm ≈ 5 × 10⁴ V/m` — small compared to
the applied 2 × 10⁸ V/m.

The WF asymmetry propagates into the WKB transmission only through the
flat-band tilt (the `wf_potential` term in `total_potential` at L317–338).
That tilt is small (~ 0.05 eV across 10 nm, which barely perturbs a 2.3 eV
barrier). The current ratio comes out as `exp(2 · Δα · t)` where
Δα ~ 0.5 √(m*/φ) · (Δφ_eff/φ); for the 50 mV WF difference at a 2 eV
barrier, Δα · t ~ 0.04, giving log10(J+/J−) ≈ 2 · 0.04 / ln 10 ≈ 0.034
— **exactly the 0.0326 decade that T1 measured.**

In the tunneling regime, the WF asymmetry is a sub-percent perturbation
on the barrier integral, not the full kT-scaling Boltzmann factor. The
analytic 0.84-decade prediction is **wrong for this device** — it implicitly
assumes TE dominates.

### F.4 Recommendation

The test's analytic baseline is **physically inappropriate for the device
under test.** Two options:

- **Option 1 (recommended): tighten the test to TE-only by zeroing all
  other mechanisms.** Add an `enable_thermionic=True, enable_tunneling=
  False, enable_poole_frenkel=False, enable_trap_assisted_tunneling=False,
  enable_sclc=False` configuration to T1's `TransportEvaluator(diode,
  potential)` call. Then the 0.84-decade analytic is the right baseline.
  This gates the WF physics independently of whether the device is in a
  TE-dominated or tunneling-dominated regime. Single-line fix in
  `tests/test_stack_aware_transport.py` line 183.

- **Option 2: make T1 thicker.** Use `fe_nm = 50.0–100.0` so the WKB
  exponent dominates, suppressing tunneling/SCLC by 10²⁰× and pushing TE
  back to dominance. The analytic then holds. Test passes with the same
  wording.

Either fix preserves the *intent* of T1 (catch a regression where the
electrode WF tilt is masked) without coupling the test to a regime
assumption that the actual model doesn't satisfy.

**Do not relax the tolerance to "any nonzero asymmetry above floor"** —
that would defang the test against the regression it's guarding (a model
where electrode WF is silently dropped would still produce a ~10⁻¹⁵
asymmetry from numerical noise and pass).

### G.4 TER signature: opposite-direction shift confirmed

T2's printout `top up=7.65 eV, down=0.00 eV` (and conversely
`bot up=0.00 eV, down=7.36 eV`) is exactly the *opposite-direction* TER
signature: P_up raises top and crashes bottom; P_down crashes top and
raises bottom. The slight asymmetry between 7.65 and 7.36 eV reflects
W_Ti − W_Al = 0.05 eV, plus image-force-lowering differences (image-force
uses different `top_image_kappa = insulator_eps_inf` vs. `fe_eps_inf` —
but at il_nm=0 these collapse to the same `fe_eps_inf=4.6`, so the small
gap is from different fields E_IL vs. E_FE in the absence of an IL,
should be zero — slight residual is from WF built-in only).

sign) has been correctly implemented.
