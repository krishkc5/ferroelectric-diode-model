## A. DIMENSIONAL CONSISTENCY

Each numbered equation in `polarization_barrier_coupling.md` is reduced in SI base units.
Constants: `ε0 [F/m] = [C/(V·m)] = [C²/(J·m)]`, `q [C]`, `E [V/m]`, `σ [C/m²]`, `P [C/m²]`,
`λ_TF [m]`, `t [m]`, `V [V]`. Goal: confirm both sides match.

### A.1  Eq. 1, Eq. 2 — bound surface charge

> Eq. 1: `σ_pol,top = P · (−ẑ) = −P            [C/m²]`
> Eq. 2: `σ_pol,bot = P · (+ẑ) = +P            [C/m²]`

`P` has units [C/m²], dotted with a unit vector → [C/m²]. **PASS.**

### A.2  Eq. 3 — charge neutrality

> `σ_pol,top + σ_pol,bot = 0`

`[C/m²] + [C/m²] = [C/m²]`, equates to 0. **PASS.**

### A.3  Eq. 4 — screening voltage

> `ΔV_screen = σ_pol · λ_TF / (ε0 · κ_∞_metal)         [V]`

`[C/m²] · [m] / ([C/(V·m)] · 1) = [C/m] / [C/(V·m)] = [V]`. **PASS.**

### A.4  Eq. 5, Eq. 6 — Schottky barrier shifts (the key check)

> Eq. 5: `Δφ_B,top = + (σ_pol,top) · λ_TF,top · q / (ε0 · κ_∞,Ti)        [J]`
> Eq. 6: `Δφ_B,bot = − (σ_pol,bot) · λ_TF,bot · q / (ε0 · κ_∞,Al)        [J]`

`[C/m²] · [m] · [C] / ([C/(V·m)] · 1) = [C²/m] / [C/(V·m)] = [C·V] = [J]`. **PASS.**

The doc states immediately above (line 79): "Δφ\_B = q · ΔV\_screen (in [J], or numerically equal in
[eV] if dropping the q factor and reading ΔV\_screen in [V])." That parenthetical is correct only if
"in [eV]" is read as "in eV when the numerical value of q (in coulombs) is omitted **and** ΔV\_screen
is taken in V." The notation is loose but not wrong. **PASS, but minor remediation:** the contract
should say "Δφ\_B^{eff} [eV] = ΔV\_screen [V] (numerically, with q stripped)" rather than the
ambiguous "numerically equal in [eV]" — the implementation team will misread this.

### A.5  Eq. 7 — local coordinate

> `x = z − b,    x ∈ [0, t_FE]`

`[m] = [m] − [m]`. **PASS.**

### A.6  Eq. 8 — depolarizing potential energy

> `ΔU_pol(x) = − (σ_pol,bot / ε_FE) · q · x         [J]`

`[C/m²] / ([C/(V·m)] · 1) = [V/m]`; `[V/m] · [C] · [m] = [C·V] = [J]`. **PASS.**

(`ε_FE = ε0 · κ_FE` per line 116, in F/m; the ratio σ/ε_FE gives V/m, i.e. a field magnitude. Fine.)

### A.7  Eq. 9 — barrier shape

> `U_FE(x) = U_FE^{(0)} − q E_FE x + ΔU_pol(x)       [J]`

`[J] = [J] − [C][V/m][m] + [J] = [J] − [J] + [J]`. **PASS.**

### A.8  Eq. 10 — displacement-field continuity

> `ε_IL E_IL − ε_FE E_FE = σ_pol,top = −P        [C/m²]`

`[F/m][V/m] = [C/(V·m)][V/m] = [C/m²]`. RHS = [C/m²]. **PASS.**

### A.9  Eq. 11 — voltage sum

> `V_app = E_IL · t_IL + E_FE · t_FE             [V]`

`[V/m][m] = [V]`. **PASS.**

### A.10  Eq. 12, Eq. 13 — partition fields

> Eq. 12: `E_FE  = [ ε_IL · V_app + P · t_IL ] / [ ε_IL · t_FE + ε_FE · t_IL ]    [V/m]`
> Eq. 13: `E_IL  = [ ε_FE · V_app − P · t_FE ] / [ ε_IL · t_FE + ε_FE · t_IL ]    [V/m]`

Numerator: `[F/m][V] + [C/m²][m] = [F·V/m] + [C/m] = [C/m] + [C/m] = [C/m]`.
Denominator: `[F/m][m] = [F]`.
Ratio: `[C/m] / [F] = [C/m] / [C/V] = [V/m]`. **PASS.**

The denominator `ε_IL · t_FE + ε_FE · t_IL` is the **standard** partition denominator (cf. Sze & Ng
Eq. 51 and the Tagantsev–Stolichnov–Setter formulation for FE/dielectric stacks). **PASS** on the
partition denominator — this is the place where contracts most often go wrong; it is correct here.

### A.11  Eq. 14 — P → 0 limit

> `E_FE → ε_IL V_app / (ε_IL t_FE + ε_FE t_IL)`
> `E_IL → ε_FE V_app / (ε_IL t_FE + ε_FE t_IL)`

Direct substitution P=0 in Eq. 12, 13 reproduces Eq. 14. **PASS.**

(Cross-check: ratio E\_IL/E\_FE = ε\_FE/ε\_IL, so V\_FE/V\_IL = (ε\_FE t\_FE / ε\_IL)·(ε\_IL/ε\_FE) =
t\_FE — wait, careful. V\_FE = E\_FE t\_FE = ε\_IL t\_FE V\_app / D, V\_IL = E\_IL t\_IL = ε\_FE t\_IL V\_app / D.
Ratio V\_FE/V\_IL = (ε\_IL t\_FE)/(ε\_FE t\_IL). The text on line 159 states this exactly. **PASS.**)

### A.12  Eq. 15 — image-force lowering

> `Δφ_IF = sqrt( q³ E / (4 π ε0 κ_∞) )         [J]`

Inside the sqrt: `[C³][V/m] / ([F/m]·1) = [C³·V/m] / [C/(V·m)] = [C²·V²] = [J²]`.
Sqrt → `[J]`. **PASS.** The unit factor under the sqrt is the textbook Schottky form.

### A.13  PF Frenkel lowering — the π / 4π check

The contract states (§7, "PF" row): `Δφ_PF = sqrt(q³ E_FE / (π ε0 κ_∞,FE))` (note `π`, not `4π`).

This is the standard Frenkel relation: in PF the Coulomb interaction is between a trapped electron
and the *donor*/trap (single charge) — not an image charge — so the prefactor under the sqrt is `π`,
which is exactly **2× larger** than the Schottky `4π` form (factor of 2 in `Δφ`, since
`sqrt(4/1) = 2`). Sze 3rd ed. Eq. 137 confirms this. **PASS** on the π. **PASS** on the
`κ_∞,FE` argument (electronic response only; the carrier transit time across the
Frenkel-radius region is too short for ionic relaxation, same argument as for image-force).

**A summary verdict:** All numbered equations dimensionally consistent. **PASS, with one
remediation note** (A.4 wording).

## B. SIGN CONVENTION TRACE

Walk the sign of σ\_pol, P\_FE, E\_FE, λ\_TF, Δφ\_B from the contract through the (claimed)
transport code paths to the final J.

### B.1  Outward-normal signs of σ\_pol,top vs σ\_pol,bot

P-SIGN §2 (lines 44–58):

> "Top FE face (FE/IL interface, z = b):  `n̂_out,top = −ẑ`."
> "Bottom FE face (FE/Al interface, z = c): `n̂_out,bot = +ẑ`."
> Eq. 1: `σ_pol,top = −P`.   Eq. 2: `σ_pol,bot = +P`.

**Test.** With `P > 0` (P along +z, from top toward bottom), the FE bound charges are positive on
the bottom face (where P points out of the FE) and negative on the top face. Eqs. 1–2 give
σ\_pol,top = −P (negative, correct) and σ\_pol,bot = +P (positive, correct). The two are equal and
opposite, satisfying Eq. 3. **The outward-normal convention is internally consistent and
correctly produces opposite-sign σ\_pol on the two faces.**

**PASS.**

### B.2  Sign-flip test on Eq. 5/6 → flat-band tilt

Substituting Eq. 1 into Eq. 5 and Eq. 2 into Eq. 6:

```
P > 0 :  Δφ_B,top = +(−|P|)·λ_TF,top·q/(ε0·κ_∞,Ti)  =  −|P|·λ_TF,top·q/(ε0·κ_∞,Ti)   (lowered)
         Δφ_B,bot = −(+|P|)·λ_TF,bot·q/(ε0·κ_∞,Al)  =  −|P|·λ_TF,bot·q/(ε0·κ_∞,Al)   (lowered)

P < 0 :  both barriers raised by the same magnitude.
```

The sign-table on lines 90–94 says exactly this: "Top barrier **lowered**, bottom barrier
**lowered**". This is **physically wrong as stated for the FED signature**: in a TER picture, the
two interfaces should move in **opposite** directions when P flips, not the same direction. The
"same direction" result here is actually correct *for the Tsymbal–Kohlstedt screening dipole alone*,
because each face independently has its own sign rule (electron barrier lowered when positive bound
charge faces the metal; raised when negative bound charge faces the metal). With σ\_pol,top = −P
*and* σ\_pol,bot = +P, the two bound-charge signs are opposite, so the dipole shifts at the two
interfaces have **opposite signs** (top barrier raised, bottom lowered for `P > 0`).

**This is a contradiction.** Let me re-derive Eq. 5/6 carefully.

The electron Schottky barrier is `φ_B = W_metal − χ_dielectric` plus a screening-dipole correction.
Per the contract's verbal sign rule (line 81): "the electron barrier `φ_B^eff` is **lowered** when
the FE face accumulates **positive** bound charge against the metal." So:

- Top interface, σ\_pol,top = −P: for `P > 0`, σ\_pol,top < 0 → **negative** bound charge against
  the Ti electrode → top barrier **raised**, i.e. `Δφ_B,top > 0` for `P > 0`.
- Bottom interface, σ\_pol,bot = +P: for `P > 0`, σ\_pol,bot > 0 → **positive** bound charge against
  the Al electrode → bottom barrier **lowered**, i.e. `Δφ_B,bot < 0` for `P > 0`.

That gives **opposite** shifts at the two interfaces — the TER signature.

But the explicit Eq. 5 says: `Δφ_B,top = + (σ_pol,top) · λ_TF · q / (ε0 · κ_∞,Ti)`. For `P > 0`,
σ\_pol,top = −P, so Eq. 5 gives Δφ\_B,top = −|P|·... = **negative**, i.e. lowered. That contradicts
the verbal rule above. The sign in Eq. 5 needs to be **negative**, not positive:

```
Δφ_B,top  =  − (σ_pol,top) · λ_TF,top · q / (ε0 · κ_∞,Ti)
          =  −(−|P|)·... = +|P|·...      [raised, for P > 0]   ← consistent with verbal rule
```

Equivalently Eq. 6 sign needs to be **positive**, not negative.

**FAIL.** The signs in Eq. 5 and Eq. 6 are flipped relative to the verbal physics rule on line 81
("electron barrier lowered when positive bound charge faces the metal"). The sign-table in lines
90–94 then *propagates* this flipped sign by stating both barriers are lowered for `P > 0`, which is
the wrong physics — that's a TER killer (a single-sign shift is a flat translation, not the
asymmetric-barrier signature of a ferroelectric tunnel junction).

**Required remediation.** Either:
  (a) flip the signs in Eq. 5 and Eq. 6 (and update the sign table on lines 90–94 to read "top
      raised, bottom lowered" for `P > 0`); **or**
  (b) keep Eq. 5/6 as written and rewrite the verbal sign rule on line 81 to say "electron barrier
      **raised** when positive bound charge faces the metal" — but that is *wrong physics*: positive
      bound charge attracts negative screening electrons in the metal, raises the local metal-side
      electrostatic potential, **lowers** the conduction-band edge of the dielectric on that side,
      hence **lowers** the electron Schottky barrier.

Option (a) is the correct fix.

**Cross-check against P-SIGN's own §3 "Note on the asymmetric tunnel barrier picture" (lines 95):**
that note says "switching the sign of P flips both shifts, but their **difference** … is what drives
TER." That is correct as a description of what *should* happen (the difference flips sign with P),
but is consistent with the verbal sign rule on line 81 only if Eq. 5 and Eq. 6 carry **opposite**
overall signs (so that fed σ\_pol,top = −P and σ\_pol,bot = +P with opposite Eq. 5/6 prefactors
yields *same-sign* shifts — which is what the sign-table claims and which is what's wrong).

The contract is internally inconsistent: the verbal rule (line 81), the sign-table (lines 90–94),
and Eq. 5/6 cannot all be right simultaneously. I read the verbal rule as the physical anchor, and
mark Eq. 5/6 + sign-table as the bug.

**Severity.** This is the single highest-impact finding in this audit. If the implementation team
codes Eq. 5/6 verbatim, the FED current will be flat-translated, not asymmetrically tilted, and the
ON/OFF ratio of the diode will collapse to ~1.

### B.3  Polarization-induced flat-band tilt: branch test (P-SIGN §4)

Eq. 8: `ΔU_pol(x) = −(σ_pol,bot/ε_FE)·q·x = −(P/ε_FE)·q·x` for `P > 0`.

For `P > 0`, ΔU\_pol(x) decreases linearly with x (drops from 0 at x=0 to `−(P t\_FE q)/ε\_FE` at
x=t\_FE). The text on line 116 says: "with the sign of Eq. 8, P > 0 produces a band edge that
**decreases linearly with x** (drops from top to bottom), consistent with §1 (positive bound charge
at the bottom face attracts electrons → lower electron potential energy on the bottom)." That is
**physically correct**: positive σ\_pol,bot lowers the electron CB at the bottom, drawing electrons
toward the bottom face. **PASS** on Eq. 8.

For `P < 0`: σ\_pol,bot = −|P|, so Eq. 8 gives ΔU\_pol(x) = +(|P|/ε\_FE)·q·x — band edge **rises**
toward the bottom. Symmetric, correct. **PASS.**

### B.4  Sign of E\_FE at V\_app = 0 (depolarizing field)

Eq. 12 with V\_app = 0: `E_FE = P·t_IL / (ε_IL t_FE + ε_FE t_IL)`. Sign of E\_FE matches sign of P.

For `P > 0`, E\_FE > 0 (along +z, top→bottom). Inside the FE, an electron in a +z field has force
−qE in the −z direction; potential energy `−qE\_FE·x` decreases with x — same direction as the
ΔU\_pol(x) drop in §B.3. **The depolarizing-field tilt and the σ\_pol bound-charge tilt point the
same way.** This is correct and consistent across Eq. 8/9 and Eq. 12.

**PASS** on the field-tilt sign in P-SIGN §4–5.

   **Re-derive cleanly.** The screening-dipole at one face produces a potential drop
   `ΔV = σ·λ_TF/(ε0·κ_∞)` *across the metal screening region*. If σ > 0 (positive bound
   charge against the metal), the bulk metal is at *higher* potential than the metal/dielectric
   interface — i.e. the metal-side electrostatic potential is **raised**, the electron
   energy at the metal Fermi level relative to the dielectric CB is **decreased**, the
   electron Schottky barrier `φ_B = E_CB,diel − E_F,metal` is **lowered**.

   Therefore for *both* the top and bottom interfaces, the dipole-shift convention is the
   *same*: Δφ\_B = − σ\_pol · λ\_TF · q / (ε0 · κ\_∞). Substituting σ\_pol,top = −P and
   σ\_pol,bot = +P:
   - Δφ\_B,top = −(−P)·λ\_TF,top·q/(ε0·κ\_∞,Ti) = +P·λ\_TF,top·q/(ε0·κ\_∞,Ti).
   - Δφ\_B,bot = −(+P)·λ\_TF,bot·q/(ε0·κ\_∞,Al) = −P·λ\_TF,bot·q/(ε0·κ\_∞,Al).

   For `P > 0`: top **raised** (+), bottom **lowered** (−). **Opposite** signs. **TER signature
   present.** This is the correct derivation.

   So the canonical contract is:
   ```
   Δφ_B,top = − (σ_pol,top) · λ_TF,top · q / (ε0 · κ_∞,Ti)        [J]   (Eq. 5')
   Δφ_B,bot = − (σ_pol,bot) · λ_TF,bot · q / (ε0 · κ_∞,Al)        [J]   (Eq. 6')
   ```
   *Both with a leading minus sign* (uniform sign convention; the asymmetry comes from σ\_pol
