## What Wave 3 (C) — diagnosis — found

Per-mechanism breakdown of the Wave-3 default fit (figure: `docs/figures/wave3_mechanism_breakdown.png`):

```
   V    |Jexp|     |Jtot|       TE         PF         BG       dominant
+0.0   3.6e-14   8.7e-14   4.9e-25   2.2e-30   8.7e-14   BG
-3.0   2.5e-13   3.0e-12   6.3e-24   3.0e-25   3.0e-12   BG
-6.0   2.0e-12   5.8e-12   1.3e-23   2.2e-24   5.8e-12   BG
-9.0   1.4e-10   8.6e-12   1.9e-24   2.9e-24   8.6e-12   BG
+5.0   3.5e-10   3.2e-10   3.1e-10   1.3e-32   4.7e-12   TE
+8.0   1.1e-08   1.3e-08   1.3e-08   3.0e-26   7.6e-12   TE
```

**At every voltage except |V| > +5, the entire model current is Background conductance.** TE is suppressed by 30+ orders of magnitude because the mean barriers are 2–3 eV. PF is essentially zero because E_FE is small at low V. The fit converged to `log10_bg_cond = 1.49` (≈ 31 A/m²/V) — a phenomenological resistor that fills in everything outside the high-bias TE regime. This is not physics; it is a fudge channel.

## What Wave 3 (B) — Tung barrier inhomogeneity — found

**Implementation:** Added `te_barrier_sigma_top_ev` and `te_barrier_sigma_bot_ev` to `TransportModelParameters`. The TE rate is multiplied by `exp(σ²/(2 (kT)²))` per Tung 1992 (PRB 45, 13509) and Werner-Güttler 1991 (JAP 69, 1522). For σ = 0.20 eV at 300 K this is a ~10¹³× enhancement, well within the literature range for metal/oxide barrier inhomogeneity (0.05–0.3 eV).

**Fit result (Tung-only, with σ_top/σ_bot in [0.05, 0.30] eV):**
- σ_top = 0.089 eV, σ_bot = 0.050 eV (lower bound).
- `log10_top_ti_scale` rose from −5.42 to −2.10 (the prefactor scale absorbed some TE work).
- Cost essentially unchanged (907 vs 909).
- Fit traces are visually identical to the no-Tung run.

**Why Tung alone didn't help:** the diagnosis showed BG dominates at low/moderate V because TE is too suppressed. Tung enhances TE by ~1500–10¹³×, but TE was 30 orders of magnitude too small — even Tung's enhancement leaves it below BG. The optimizer therefore prefers to leave BG dominant.

**Tung + tunneling + TAT (B's full ambition):**
Enabling tunneling and TAT in the fit (these were hardcoded off in `fit_dciv_experiment.py`) made the result much worse. The polarization-tilted WKB barrier from Wave 2 is highly sensitive at moderate V; without trap-density / effective-mass / cross-section bounds in the fit, the optimizer pushes barrier offsets and floors to their upper limits to suppress overshooting tunneling current and fails to converge (nfev=17/120, cost = 3113, mean error = 1.24).

The script default has been reverted to keep tunneling and TAT disabled. Re-enabling needs:
- Trap density and capture cross-section in `TransportModelParameters` with literature bounds.
- A proper TAT prefactor parameterization (currently a single `trap_assisted_prefactor_a_per_m2` of 5×10⁸ that the fit doesn't tune).
- Effective-mass tightening on HfO_x and AlScN (the parameter audit's row 13 already flagged the m* range issue).

## What this means

The slide-11 fit got 0.626 by allowing prefactor scales to absorb up to **9 orders of magnitude** of unmodeled physics (slide 11: `log10_pf_fe_scale = 9.87`). With Wave 2's polarization-coupled barriers in place, the prefactor scales can't absorb that much without producing wrong shape — the polarization-driven barrier asymmetry is now constrained, so the fit's effective freedom is narrower.

The remaining 0.7-decade error is the *real* physics gap. None of Tung, tunneling, or TAT close it without additional model machinery (trap-density bounds, ohmic baseline conductance, or — most likely — a proper barrier-height distribution on the TUNNELING path, not just on TE).

---

## Where to go next, ranked by likely impact

1. **Constrain the existing tunneling/TAT path before enabling it in the fit.** The Wave-2 polarization-tilted WKB is more sensitive than the old static trapezoid, and the fit's optimizer cannot handle it without bounds on m_eff and trap density. ~1 day of work; would let TAT kick in at low/moderate V.
2. **Tung distribution on the tunneling channel, not just TE.** Real interfaces have barrier inhomogeneity that affects WKB transmission, not just thermionic activation. The integral form is more complex (need to integrate the WKB exponent, not just the prefactor exponent), but the literature exists (Sullivan et al., JAP 70, 7403 (1991)).
3. **Spatial barrier-height distribution on the FE itself.** AlScN polycrystalline with Sc fluctuations gives barrier patches; modeled as a parallel sum of Schottky diodes with Gaussian φ_B distribution (Tung patchy interface).
4. **Cross-validate the Liu Nat. Commun. 13:1009 (2022) benchmark.** R-BENCH's WebFetch was sandbox-denied; if we can digitize that paper's I-V, we can check whether the Wave-2/2.5 model qualitatively matches the published electroresistance ratio and zero-bias rectification of an analogous AlScN FeD.
5. **Frequency / quasistatic limit.** Real DC sweeps may have effective sweep rate slow enough that domain creep and trap charging contribute. Quasistatic Preisach can't capture those without a Landau-Khalatnikov dynamics layer.

For the predictive-design goal stated on slide 13, the model is already in a defensible state for stack-comparison studies (it correctly captures IL-thickness/material/electrode effects on hysteresis; the stack-aware coupling was just confirmed by the sanity-test suite). It should not yet be used to claim quantitative agreement with a specific measured I-V.
